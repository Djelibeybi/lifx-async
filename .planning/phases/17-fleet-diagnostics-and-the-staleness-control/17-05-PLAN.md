Purpose: R6 and R7 **as amended by SPEC amendment A3**. A control with no recorded comparison

**Read amendment A3 in `17-SPEC.md` before writing any prose in this plan.** R7 and AC-20
originally required stating that `discover()` yielding a device is not evidence the device is
reachable. That is false for the shipped library and the SPEC has been corrected.
`discover()` (`src/lifx/api.py:1058`) routes its mDNS candidates through
`_discover_verified_devices_mdns` (`src/lifx/api.py:1128`), which hands each candidate to
`_verify_mdns_candidate` (`src/lifx/network/discovery/mdns/discovery.py:1351`); that opens a
`DeviceConnection`, sends `Light.GetColor()` or a `Device.EchoRequest`, raises on a serial
mismatch, and yields only on a correlated answer. A device built from a stale advertisement
cannot reach a `discover()` caller. `find_by_serial()` verifies through the same helper, via
`_race_serial_sources` (`src/lifx/api.py:1374`). The function that can yield an unverified
advertisement is `discover_mdns()` (`src/lifx/api.py:1268`), which calls `discover_devices_mdns`
directly.

The qualitative distinction is also **already published**, in wording Phase 16 locked into the
approved-phrase tuple: `docs/user-guide/discovery.md` says an mDNS result "proves an
advertisement exists, not that the advertised device is currently reachable", and points at
`discover()`, "whose mDNS candidates must answer a correlated device request before they are
yielded". So what this phase adds is not the distinction. It is the measured durations on both
sides of it, plus the restoration-delay direction, which is genuinely undocumented.

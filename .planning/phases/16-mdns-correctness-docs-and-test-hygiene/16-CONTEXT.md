## Implementation Decisions

### Owner-name normalisation (R1)

- **D-01:** `selected_address_for()` becomes a thin public wrapper that normalises its owner
  argument through `_normalise_dns_name()` and delegates to a private method taking an
  already-normalised owner. The two internal callers, `discovery.py:788` and
  `discovery.py:845`, call the private method directly.

  Rationale: `_normalise_dns_name()` strips **exactly one** trailing dot, so it is not
  idempotent (`"host.."` becomes `"host."`, then `"host"`). Both internal callers pass a
  `target` already normalised once at `discovery.py:659`. A naive one-line
  `owner = _normalise_dns_name(owner)` inside `selected_address_for()` would therefore
  double-normalise on the internal path and turn a currently-resolving multi-trailing-dot
  name into a permanent pending-target miss: `addresses_for(target)` at line 787 would still
  return addresses while `ip` came back `None`, leaving the instance pending and re-queried
  forever. The public/private split makes the normalisation contract explicit at the
  signature rather than relying on every caller knowing it.
  **Reversibility:** reversible. Both methods are private to the module and the public
  signature is unchanged.

- **D-02:** `_normalise_dns_name()` is **not** changed to strip all trailing dots. Making the
  helper idempotent was considered and rejected: it is called on the ingest path at
  `discovery.py:400` and on every SRV target at `659`, so widening it changes cache keying
  for every caller to fix a problem that D-01 confines to one method.

- **D-03:** The four fail-closed guard checks currently duplicated between
  `selected_address_for()` (`discovery.py:350-357`) and `pending_targets()`
  (`discovery.py:841-846`) are extracted into one private predicate used by both.

  Rationale: the duplication is the structural cause of the defect. `pending_targets()` and
  `selected_address_for()` assert the same unusable-owner condition, and only one of them was
  updated when normalisation was introduced. One predicate means the next guard added lands
  in both places.
  **Reversibility:** reversible. A private extraction inside one module.

- **D-04:** The parametrised invariant test drives the **three public lookups only**
  (`records_for`, `addresses_for`, `selected_address_for`) across bare, trailing-dot,
  uppercase and mixed-case owner forms. It does not reach `_owner_is_unusable` or the private
  delegate directly, so a later refactor of the private helpers does not break it.

  **Known residual:** with D-03 extracting a predicate that `pending_targets()` also uses, and
  D-04 declining to drive `pending_targets()` through the owner-form matrix, the dedup's
  equivalence for that method rests on the existing suite rather than on a new assertion. The
  planner should confirm the existing `pending_targets()` coverage exercises each of the four
  guard branches before treating the extraction as proven.

### Import-sweep enforcement (R4)

- **D-05:** `PLC0415` (`import-outside-top-level`) is added to ruff's `select` list, with
  `[tool.ruff.lint.per-file-ignores]` entries for `src/**`, `.planning/**`,
  `tests/test_animation/**`, `tests/test_theme/**` and
  `tests/test_devices/test_multizone.py`.

  Rationale: the rule already exists in ruff, so a bespoke contract test would duplicate a
  linter the repository already runs in pre-commit. A repository-wide enable is not available
  to this phase: `src/` carries 82 violations that are largely legitimate circular-import
  breakers (`discovery.py:872-878` imports the six device classes inside
  `_create_device_from_record`; `api.py:1322` imports `discover_devices_mdns`), `.planning/`
  carries 49, and 23 sit in files this phase must not edit. Scoping by per-file-ignore makes
  the swept tree permanently protected while leaving each excluded path a visible, deletable
  entry: **Phases 18 and 19 delete their own ignore line when they sweep their files.** That
  handoff is the reason this shape was chosen over selecting `PLC0415` on `tests/` alone.
  **Reversibility:** costly. Undoing means removing the rule, the five ignore entries, and
  every `noqa` comment D-06 introduces, in one change.

  **D-05 amended during plan-phase: five entries become seven.** Planning measured the rule
  rather than reasoning about it, and `ruff check --select PLC0415 .` reports two violations
  outside all five enumerated paths, in a git-tracked archived spike harness duplicated at
  `.agents/skills/.../probe.py:374` and `.claude/skills/.../probe.py:374`. With five entries
  `uv run ruff check .` reports 168, so requirement 4's acceptance criterion is unsatisfiable
  as written. `.agents/skills/**` and `.claude/skills/**` are therefore added as a sixth and
  seventh per-file-ignore entry, on the same ground D-07 already permits: a path this phase is
  forbidden to edit. Verified: with seven entries `ruff check --no-cache .` reports exactly the
  166 in-scope sweep violations and nothing else. The alternatives — sweeping the two archived
  probes into scope, or adding a ruff `exclude` list that would silence every rule on those
  paths rather than one — were both put to the operator and declined. The decision's shape,
  rationale, handoff property and `costly` reversibility rating are unchanged; only the
  enumeration widened.

- **D-06:** A retained function-local import is marked with
  `# noqa: PLC0415` carrying an inline reason naming the circular-import or patching
  constraint. A bare `noqa` with no reason is not acceptable.

- **D-07:** SPEC prohibition P6 (`MUST NOT add a noqa or per-file-ignore to make the import
  sweep pass`) is bounded as follows, so `verify-phase` can distinguish a legitimate mark from
  a suppression:
  - **Permitted:** a `noqa: PLC0415` that carries a named circular-import or patching reason;
    a per-file-ignore covering a path this phase is forbidden to edit (the five in D-05).
  - **Prohibited:** a bare `noqa` with no reason; any per-file-ignore covering a file that is
    in scope for the sweep; any broadening of an existing ignore to swallow an in-scope file.
  - The permitted set is enumerable: five per-file-ignore entries, plus however many
    reason-bearing `noqa` comments the sweep proves necessary. A verifier can list them.

- **D-08:** The sweep is **one commit**, with the ruff configuration change as a separate
  commit. Per-directory or per-requirement splits were declined: the sweep is mechanical and
  a bisect landing on all 27 files at once is acceptable for a change with no behavioural
  intent.
  **Reversibility:** reversible. A single mechanical commit reverts cleanly.

### Replacement prose (R3)

- **D-13:** The sentence `Mesh scale is proven synthetically.` is **dropped outright**, in
  both `src/lifx/api.py:1287` and `docs/user-guide/discovery.md:120`. It is not replaced with
  a reworded scale claim. The existing bounded-discovery paragraph already tells the caller
  what to conclude, and a replacement claim would be one more sentence to keep true.

- **D-14:** Proxy responses gain caller-facing prose stating that a Thread border router
  advertises every device on its mesh, so an mDNS result proves an advertisement exists rather
  than that the device is currently reachable, **and** naming the next action: confirm with a
  request, or with `discover()`, whose UDP leg is unicast-verified. This directly encodes the
  Phase 14 THREAD-04 staleness finding and satisfies SPEC prohibition P4.

  The reasoning currently in the code comment at `discovery.py:782` (an instance with an SRV
  record but no address records stays pending rather than falling back to the packet source,
  because guessing would misattribute devices to the border router) was considered for
  inclusion and **not** selected. It stays a code comment.

  **D-14 amended after cross-AI plan review: the named next action was factually wrong.** The
  clause "with `discover()`, whose UDP leg is unicast-verified" does not describe the
  implementation. `discover()`'s UDP leg consumes `discover_devices_shared()` directly
  (`src/lifx/api.py:1112`) and performs no verification — a broadcast reply is yielded as found.
  It is the **mDNS** leg that verifies: `_discover_verified_devices_mdns()`
  (`src/lifx/api.py:1125`) opens a `DeviceConnection` and requires an answer to a correlated
  `Light.GetColor` or `Device.EchoRequest` before yielding
  (`src/lifx/network/discovery/mdns/discovery.py:1361`). So verification exists, and D-14 named
  the unverified leg as the verified one. The decision's intent is unchanged — proxy responses
  still gain caller-facing prose that distinguishes an advertisement from reachability and names
  a next action — but the next action is now attributed to `discover()`'s mDNS candidates
  answering a correlated device request. The error mattered rather than being a phrasing detail:
  a caller told the UDP leg is verified would trust an unverified broadcast reply for exactly the
  reachability judgement P4 exists to protect. `16-02-PLAN.md` carries the correction under
  `## Corrected verification attribution`, locks it positively with an approved-phrase fragment,
  and adds `_FORBIDDEN_VERIFICATION_ATTRIBUTION_PHRASES` so the contract cannot stay green while
  the wrong claim is published.

- **D-15:** Testing limitations are carried by **recasting** the existing fleet-specific
  paragraph in `discovery.md` into a caller-facing limitation: behaviour on a large or
  congested network is not characterised, so a caller should not size timeouts or retry policy
  from this documentation. Designating the existing paragraph unchanged was considered and
  declined; it reads as an evidence disclaimer rather than as advice. The recast must still
  satisfy SPEC prohibition P5 (no fleet-specific observation presented as a universal
  benchmark).

- **D-16:** New approved-phrase entries are **short distinctive fragments**, matching the
  existing style (`does not join the multicast group`,
  `does not authenticate or correlate responders`). Full-sentence locks were declined because
  Phase 20's em-dash recast of these same pages would break every entry; topic-keyword-only
  locks were declined as too weak to guarantee meaning.

## Specific Ideas

- The naive one-line fix was explicitly rejected once the non-idempotence of
  `_normalise_dns_name()` was surfaced. The operator chose the structural split knowing it is
  a larger diff than the requirement's literal wording implies.
- Guard-check duplication was treated as in scope because it is the same fail-closed boundary
  MDNS-09 names, not as an opportunistic refactor.
- The scale claim was dropped rather than reworded. The operator's framing throughout was that
  a sentence a caller cannot act on should not be replaced with a better-worded sentence a
  caller cannot act on.
- The testing-limitations paragraph is to be recast into advice ("do not size timeouts or
  retry policy from this documentation"), not preserved as an evidence disclaimer.
- Per-file-ignores were accepted specifically because each excluded path becomes a visible
  line another phase deletes, making the handoff to Phases 18 and 19 explicit rather than
  implicit.

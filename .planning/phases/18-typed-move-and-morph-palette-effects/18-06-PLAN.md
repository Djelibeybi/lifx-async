<objective>
Add `MultiZoneLight.set_move_effect()`, the one-call typed Move method, and give Move visible
colours the way the LIFX app does.

Purpose: EFFECT-01 R5 and R7 (the method and its runnable example) and EFFECT-02 R9. Move carries
no palette on the wire; it rotates the zone colours already shown, so a single-colour strip shows
no movement. The typed path paints a palette first when there is nothing to move; the raw
`set_effect(effect)` path stays byte-for-byte unchanged (R6, D-21).

Output: `set_move_effect()` and its private palette step, the corrected `MultiZoneLight` class
docstring, and the tests.

**What R9's acceptance requires.** Painting goes through `apply_theme()` (D-20), whose
`MultiZoneGenerator` shuffles the theme and blends between its colours. That was always the
design: R9's Target says the zones are painted "through `apply_theme()`", and the maintainer
amended R9's acceptance text in 92a657d to match it. The single-colour case therefore requires
that `apply_theme()` receives the three R10 colours at `duration=0` and that
`get_all_color_zones()` then holds `MultiZoneGenerator`'s shuffled blend of those colours; an
explicit palette is passed to `apply_theme()` unchanged, and `set_move_effect()` does not call
`get_all_color_zones()` (the SPEC's "with no zone read"; `apply_theme()` itself still awaits
`get_zone_count()` and `get_power()` before painting, which R9 does not forbid). The tests below
prove exactly that: the `apply_theme()` call and its `Theme` colours, and a zone read-back equal
to the generator's blend of the three colours for one of their six orders.

**Direction is read from the received packet.** As in plan 03, the locked lifx-emulator-core
3.7.0's GetEffect reports zeroed Move parameters (verified against the installed package), so
`get_effect()` supplies the effect type and speed and the captured `MultiZone.SetEffect` supplies
the direction, as the amended 18-SPEC R5 acceptance states for the same 3.7.0 release. The
executor does not edit the SPEC.

**Fixtures and packet logs on 3.7.0.** Every emulator test here uses fixtures that exist today in
`tests/conftest.py`; no IPv6 endpoint and no Thread device is involved, and nothing in this plan
adds either. The planner verified each fact below on a live in-process 3.7.0 server on 2026-09-23:
- `emulator_devices[4]` is `d073d5000005` at 127.0.0.1: emulator product 38 (LIFX Beam), 80
  zones, firmware 3.70 with extended multizone, so `get_all_color_zones()` sends
  GetExtendedColorZones (511) and `set_all_color_zones()` paints with SetExtendedColorZones (510).
- `emulator_server` yields `(port, server, scenario_manager)`; plan 03's
  `_received_packets(server, serial, pkt_type=None)` wraps `server.get_device(serial).process_packet`
  and yields `(header, packet)` tuples in arrival order. Every capture block closes with a
  `get_effect()` (507) round trip, because the emulator sends an acknowledged SET's fast-path ack
  before it calls `process_packet()`. A packet dropped by a scenario never reaches
  `process_packet()`, so it never appears in a capture.
- The `scenario_manager` fixture is a callable context manager that applies a per-device
  `ScenarioConfig` (for example `{"drop_packets": {511: 1.0, 502: 1.0}}`), invalidates the scenario
  caches, and clears both on exit.
- `switch_device` is a `DeviceConnection` (with `.serial`, `.ip` and `.port`) to the product-70
  switch `d073d5000200`, which answers StateUnhandled to every multizone packet; a
  `MultiZoneLight` pointed at it raises `LifxUnsupportedCommandError` from `set_effect()`, and the
  capture records the 508 it answered, because the StateUnhandled reply is built inside
  `process_packet()`.
</objective>
